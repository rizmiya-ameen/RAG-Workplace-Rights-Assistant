Report a psychosocial hazard

Psychosocial hazards can increase the risk of psychological or physical harm. This page explains how to report a psychosocial hazard and where to get support.

What are psychosocial hazards

Psychosocial hazards are a serious occupational health and safety issue.

Psychosocial hazards are factors in the design, management, or social interactions at work that can cause psychological harm, physical harm, or both.

Common psychosocial hazards include bullying, aggression or violence, sexual harassment and high or low job demands.

These are some examples. Every workplace is unique, so psychosocial hazards can look different, depending on the work being done, the workplace environment, and the people involved.

If you’re concerned about a psychosocial hazard at work, there are several ways to report this.

Psychosocial hazards in the workplace  

WorkSafe’s role

When a hazard is reported to WorkSafe, we assess the information to decide whether further steps will be taken.

What WorkSafe can do

WorkSafe can help address psychological health and safety concerns. We can:

  • provide information about occupational health and safety obligations
  • investigate incidents and complaints, where required.

What WorkSafe can’t do

WorkSafe cannot:

  • act on behalf of an employee, employer or other party
  • direct disciplinary action or termination of employment
  • investigate matters outside of Victoria
  • provide legal advice or legal representation
  • mediate disputes or take sides
  • provide counselling or psychological support.

Before you contact WorkSafe

Before contacting WorkSafe, we recommend you report the hazard to someone at your workplace if you feel safe to do so. In many cases, this approach may address and resolve the issue. If your workplace doesn’t know about the issue, it may limit how WorkSafe can help.

Your workplace should have reporting options for psychosocial hazards, such as:

  • a hazard or incident reporting system
  • policies or procedures that cover how to prevent and respond to hazards and risks.

You can raise your concerns by having a conversation with a:

  • manager, supervisor or HR department
  • health and safety representative (HSR)
  • trusted person in the organisation.

How to report a psychosocial hazard to WorkSafe

If an issue can’t be resolved at a workplace level, you can contact WorkSafe’s Psychosocial Advisory Team.

The experienced team can:

  • help you understand your workplace psychological health and safety rights and responsibilities
  • provide advice and guidance about psychosocial hazards
  • help you lodge a WorkCover claim for a mental injury
  • refer matters to a WorkSafe inspector to make further enquiries.

Contact the Psychosocial Advisory Team

Before you call, it's helpful to have information about:

  • what happened
  • when and where it happened
  • who was involved
  • if the issue has been reported to the workplace, and if so, what the outcome was.

What happens next

WorkSafe will assess the information you provide and decide if further action is needed, which may involve sending an inspector to the workplace.

Workplace inspections  

Please note, contacting WorkSafe does not automatically result in an investigation or trigger a WorkCover claim.

Reporting anonymously or on behalf of someone else

You can report a hazard anonymously or on behalf of another person. 

If you are reporting on behalf of someone else, you should get written consent from the person affected. 

When a report is made anonymously or without the affected persons consent, this may limit WorkSafe’s ability to gather information, investigate the issue and keep you updated on progress. 

Other agencies that may be able to help you

WorkSafe’s role is to help keep Victoria’s workplaces healthy and safe. Depending on your situation, another agency may be better placed to help.

  1. Fair Work Ombudsman

    Fair Work Ombudsman

    The Fair Work Ombudsman helps employees and employers understand workplace rights and obligations under the Fair Work Act.

    It can assist with issues such as:

    • pay and wages
    • performance management
    • unfair dismissal
    • leave entitlements
    • workplace conditions
    • other employment rights.

    Phone: 13 13 94

  2. Fair Work Commission

    Fair Work Commission

    The Fair Work Commission is Australia's national workplace relations tribunal.

    • makes and varies awards
    • approves enterprise agreements
    • helps resolve workplace disputes.

    Phone: 1300 799 675

  3. Victorian Equal Opportunity and Human Rights Commission (VEOHRC)

    Victorian Equal Opportunity and Human Rights Commission (VEOHRC)

    VEOHRC helps people understand and resolve issues related to:

    • discrimination
    • sexual harassment
    • victimisation
    • human rights.

    Phone: 1300 292 153

  4. Workforce Inspectorate Victoria

    Workforce Inspectorate Victoria 

    Workforce Inspectorate Victoria promotes and enforces Victoria's laws relating to:

    • long service leave
    • child employment
    • wage theft
    • owner drivers
    • forestry contractors.

    Phone: 1300 366 356

  5. The Office of the Victorian Information Commissioner (OVIC)

    The Office of the Victorian Information Commissioner (OVIC)

    OVIC assist with matters relating to privacy and data breaches.

    Phone: 1300 006 842

  6. Victoria Police

    Victoria Police 

    Victoria Police respond to criminal matters and emergencies.

    They can:

    • respond to immediate risks
    • investigate criminal offences
    • take action where criminal laws have been broken.

Where to find immediate help

If you feel unsafe right now or need immediate support, the following services are available 24 hours per day, 7 days a week.

  • Victoria Police on 000
  • Lifeline on 13 11 14
  • Victorian Suicide Line on 1300 651 251
  • 1800RESPECT (national sexual assault hotline) on 1800 737 732
  • Sexual Assault Crisis Line on 1800 806 292

You may also consider speaking with your healthcare provider.
